# The White Room — Daniel's Voice Script & Acting Direction (Audio step A4)

> Source: AudioPlan §3.3 lines, D-020 (Daniel), D-024 (inner monologue), D-030 / D-215 (tone mapping), D-031 (recorded locally).
> **37 takes + 1 room tone.** File format: WAV, 48 kHz, 24-bit, mono, peaks ≈ −3 dBFS, ~0.3 s silence at head and tail.
> If you change any wording while recording, update the **Line** column here: the subtitle and localization text must match the audio exactly.

## Who Daniel is (baseline for every take)
- Millennial office worker in his early 30s, underpaid and tired. Dry, sarcastic humour is his defence. He is **not** a hero and **not** a narrator; he is talking to himself.
- **Inner monologue:** close to the mic (~15–20 cm, use a pop filter), quieter than normal speech, a little breathy. **Never project.** Even panic stays under the breath, so whisper-panic rather than shout.
- Use your natural voice, slightly lower and slower than usual. Don't "do a character".

## The three tones
| Tone | When the game uses it | How it sounds |
|---|---|---|
| **N, Neutral** | Before the fork, and default | Conversational mutter. Honest confusion, dry humour, normal pace, endings often trail off or turn into questions. |
| **R, Resolute** | After the fork, HP ≥ 50% | Lower pitch, slower, shorter phrases, **downward** endings. Decided and focused. Humour only bone-dry. |
| **E, Erratic** | After the fork, HP < 35% | Faster, broken rhythm, pitch jumps, self-interruptions, audible breaths. Contradicts itself. Whispered, never shouted. |

## Session plan
1. Warm up for 5 minutes: hum, lip trills, read a few lines out loud.
2. Record a **10 s room tone** first (silence, mic on, you still): `vo.roomtone.wav`.
3. Record in **tone blocks** so you stay in one headspace: **N** (19 takes) → **R** (8 takes) → **E** (10 takes). Leave E for last because it's the most tiring.
4. Do 2–3 takes of each line and keep the best. Say the file name out loud before each take ("intro door, take 2"); it makes editing much faster.
5. Edit: trim, remove clicks and breaths that are too loud, normalize every file to the same loudness, export with the exact file name below.
